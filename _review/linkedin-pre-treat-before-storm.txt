Most property managers judge a snow operation by what happens after the snow falls: how long the lot takes to clear and what the pavement looks like once it has been plowed. Part of that result depends on whether the lot was treated before the storm arrived.

Plowing after snow has already bonded to pavement can leave more residue than treating a lot ahead of the storm. Anti-icing material applied before a forecasted snow or ice event keeps the first layer from bonding to the surface, and that can cut the time crews need to get a lot clear once plowing starts.

At Twin City Outdoor Services, where Tom Rosensteel is General Manager, anti-icing material goes down on commercial parking lots and drive lanes across the Twin Cities metro before a forecasted snow or ice event. It is one more piece of a full-season commercial snow and ice plan, and a property manager who wants lots and drive lanes treated ahead of forecasted events can ask about adding pre-treatment to this winter's contract.

Whether the lot was treated before the forecasted event is part of the plan, and it is worth raising when this winter's contract is reviewed.
