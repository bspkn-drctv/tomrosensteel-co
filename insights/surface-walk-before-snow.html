Why the Fall Surface Walk Has to Happen Before the Snow Does

Why a commercial lot has to be walked on foot before the first hard freeze, what the walk should catch on asphalt and concrete, how to split findings between this season and spring, and why the written record matters once snow covers the lot.

By Tom Rosensteel  ·  Twin City Outdoor Services

Most of the damage a Twin Cities parking lot takes over a winter starts with something a property manager could have seen in the fall. The stretch from fall into winter is the hardest part of the year on a commercial lot, because daytime melt and overnight refreeze push water into every crack in the asphalt and concrete, and each freeze and thaw cycle widens what was a hairline fracture into a pothole, a heaved slab, or a curb that no longer sheds water the way it should. Once the ground freezes solid, most structural repair options close for the season. The question is not whether the lot will be walked. It is whether it gets walked while the damage is still visible and still fixable.

Tom Rosensteel is General Manager of Twin City Outdoor Services (TCOS), which serves commercial properties across the Twin Cities metro area. TCOS handles commercial asphalt repair, concrete repair and replacement, and catch basin and drainage work under one contract across the Twin Cities, which is what lets a single fall walkthrough turn into scheduled work instead of a list split between vendors.

Why the Timing Is the Whole Point

A fall surface walk is not a drive through the lot. It means getting out of the vehicle and checking the drive lanes, parking stalls, loading areas, sidewalks, entrances and any accessible route on foot, in daylight, before the first forecasted hard freeze of the season. That timing is not a preference. It is the only time the inspection works.

Waiting until after the first snowfall means walking the property blind. Damage that was obvious on bare pavement is invisible under a couple of inches of snow, and it stays invisible until the thaw, by which point the freeze and thaw cycles have been working on it all winter. The same crack is a repair in the fall and a liability in the winter, and the only difference is whether someone saw it in time.

What the Walk Should Catch

A property rarely has only asphalt or only concrete, so the walk covers both. The things to look for:

  • Alligator cracking in drive lanes and high traffic turning areas, where interlocking cracks mean the base underneath is already failing.
  • Isolated potholes near dumpster pads, loading docks and entrance aprons, where heavier and slower moving vehicles concentrate the stress.
  • Joint and edge spalling on concrete sidewalks, curbs and the slabs next to the building, especially where water already pools after rain.
  • Standing water or ponding anywhere in the lot, which both speeds up freeze and thaw damage and points to a drainage or catch basin problem that only gets worse once snow is on the ground.
  • Curb and ramp settling at accessible entry points, which is a liability exposure now and a snowplow hazard once the area is covered.

Two Lists, Not One

The walk produces findings. What makes it useful is sorting them into two lists: what can still be repaired this season, and what has to be flagged for spring.

Asphalt and concrete do not share a deadline. Crack sealing on asphalt is weather dependent, and isolated potholes can be patched until the ground is frozen solid. Cold mix patching stretches the window later into the season, but it is a temporary fix rather than a permanent one; a pothole or crack caught before the first hard freeze can get a proper hot mix repair or crack seal that lasts through the winter instead of being revisited when the weather warms. Concrete depends on consistent cure temperatures, so a fall repair needs enough lead time for the patch or pour to cure before a hard freeze. Minor spalls can be repaired in good fall weather with the right cure time, while a heaved or cracked slab needs its replacement scheduled before the ground freezes for excavation. Drainage and catch basin issues belong on the first list too, because they are best addressed before snow cover makes inspection and access difficult.

Finding a problem early leaves room to schedule the repair. Finding it late usually means waiting for spring. The walk is what decides which of those a property manager is dealing with.

When the Answer Is Spring

Some findings will land on the second list no matter how early the walk happens. That is not a failure of the inspection. The right move is to document the problem with photos and a location note, flag it for the earliest spring repair window, and in the meantime mark or barricade it if it presents a trip or vehicle hazard under snow cover.

The written record is the part that matters most. A known, documented issue that is addressed in spring is a very different liability position from one nobody tracked. If a slip and fall or a vehicle damage claim comes up during the winter, being able to show that the surface was inspected and addressed before the ground froze is a materially stronger position than having no record at all. For multi site portfolios, a standardized checklist used at every property makes findings comparable across sites and keeps nothing from being missed because one site manager has a sharper eye than another.

A Scheduled Task, Not a Complaint Response

The property managers who avoid spring surprises are most often the ones who treat this as a scheduled fall task rather than a response to a complaint. In practice that means a documented walkthrough of every asphalt and concrete surface on the property, a prioritized list split between what can still be repaired this season and what has to wait for spring, and a single vendor who can act on both the asphalt and the concrete findings instead of coordinating two contractors on a shrinking calendar.

Once snow is on the ground, this stops being a repair conversation and becomes a snow and ice management conversation. The fall surface walk is the last point in the year where a property manager gets to choose which conversation that is.
